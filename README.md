# memoire-opensource
Hello!

I am a third-year DNMADE student researching the topic of open source for my diploma thesis in graphic design.
As part of my research, I conducted some interviews with students from 42 Nice to understand their relationship with open-source tools and culture. This repository synthesizes the recurring themes and insights that emerged from our conversations.
Feel free to read, share, open an issue, or submit a pull request if you want to add comments or corrections—your feedback is a huge help for my research. Thank you!

Key points from the interviews:

1. Code as a common good: Open source is described as a fundamental learning tool; it allows people to review others’ work, compare different approaches, and develop critical thinking skills without having to “reinvent the wheel.”

2. Transparency: Relying on open tools provides the assurance that “nothing is hidden,” unlike proprietary software, where data processing and inner workings remain opaque.

3. The Foundations of the Digital World: The vast majority of today’s infrastructure—from systems based on the Linux kernel, such as Android, to software libraries—relies entirely on open source.

4. “Thankless work”: Many developers devote thousands of volunteer hours to open-source projects out of passion or conviction, at the risk of their work being overlooked or appropriated for free by large private companies.

5. Sovereignty and resistance to control: Open-source software is seen as a democratic bulwark against the centralization of tech giants or governments, guaranteeing sustainable and shared access to knowledge.
